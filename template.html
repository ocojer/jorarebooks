About
manuscripts · archives · fine & rare books

I've been in the rare book trade for over 25 years, co-founding The Manhattan Rare Book Company in 1999. One of our first acquisitions was a 1685 Shakespeare Fourth Folio, bought sight unseen, bidding by phone at four in the morning — a Sotheby's sale in London. Our first printed catalogue, in 2001, featured that Folio alongside a first edition of Gatsby in the first state dust jacket.

These days my focus has shifted toward archives and manuscript material. Some offerings are in association with MRB; others I offer separately. I'm still buying more than I should, but less than I'd like.

What draws me is the living pulse. It's there if you let the material speak on its own terms — letters, manuscripts, documents that catch a hinge moment: a new idea, a private reckoning or revelation, a hidden fault line suddenly exposed, an artistic pivot, a newly surfaced document or archive that forces us to reconsider what we thought we knew. What strikes me every time — and I find it both eerie and thrilling — is that the people behind these documents almost never fully understood what they were part of. If you find the right material, curiosity and empathy will breathe new life into ink on paper.

I buy and sell manuscripts, documents, archives, and rare books. The best moments in this work have a distinctive quality. You are looking at something you didn't know existed. Your pulse quickens. Your mind races. "Is this what I think it is?" Sometimes the answer is yes. You feel like Indiana Jones.

But it's more than adventure. There is a responsibility in that moment, and you feel that too.

— Jeremy

Rare Book Chat

I also co-host Rare Book Chat, a podcast about the rare book trade, with Michael DiRuggiero. Michael and I have been having a good time talking about and trading rare books since last century, and we're still at it — so please give us a listen on whatever pod platform you call home.

Selected Episodes
  • A conversation with Sarah Funke Butler on an extraordinary archive of letters from Ernest Hemingway to journalist Lillian Ross, casting new light on Hemingway's attitudes toward women and his inner creative life.
  • A conversation with Adam Douglas, Senior Specialist at Peter Harrington Books, London, on their copy of the 1623 First Folio of Shakespeare. Without it, roughly half of Shakespeare's plays would have been lost entirely, including Macbeth, The Tempest, and Twelfth Night.
  • A conversation with Darren Sutherland of Bonhams on Sylvia Plath's favorite book: her heavily annotated copy of The Joy of Cooking, which she called her "beloved Rombauer."
